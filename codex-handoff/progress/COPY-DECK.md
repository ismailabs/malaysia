## Kuala Lumpur

Kuala Lumpur. A city read at two heights.
Look up for the skyline. Look closer for the city. Kuala Lumpur's towers are a recognisable silhouette, but the changes of scale make the portrait: glass above, shopfronts and sheltered counters below.
The skyline / A pause on Petaling Street / Read the city slowly / Before you go / Sources and further reading.
The essay separates the KLCC skyline from Petaling Street rather than pretending they are one continuous viewpoint. It discusses visible street details, contemporary city layers and respectful observation. It links Tourism Malaysia for changing visitor details, without inventing times, prices or a firsthand visit.

## George Town

George Town. The street is still the story.
Shutters, shaded walkways and a place to stop for food: George Town is a city best read close to the buildings. Its heritage belongs to the people using it as much as to the façades.
A living streetscape / Under the five-foot way / A small parcel of the day / Before you go / Sources and further reading.
The essay identifies Penang and the UNESCO property's shared name with Melaka; it distinguishes living heritage from empty scenery and encourages unobstructed walkways, permission for portraits and room for everyday work.

## Langkawi

Langkawi. Follow the edge of the island.
In Langkawi, the edge is where the landscape gathers: forest above, limestone at the waterline and a changing surface below. This is the coast of Kedah, at the northwest of the peninsula.
Limestone and tide / Look at the whole frame / A different sense of distance / Before you go / Sources and further reading.
The essay describes the photographed Kilim landscape, explains UNESCO Global Geopark context and the relationship of limestone and mangroves, and links the official geopark rather than inventing tours or wildlife encounters.

## Sabah

Sabah. A mountain changes the scale.
The granite skyline of Kinabalu rises above a green foreground. Elsewhere in Sabah, a forest stream brings attention back to stones, roots and running water. One state holds both kinds of looking.
Across the South China Sea / Granite above the forest / Beside a stream / Before you go / Sources and further reading.
The essay correctly treats Sabah as a state and Kinabalu Park as one part of it. The stream image is specifically at Mari Mari Cultural Village, not asserted to be inside Kinabalu Park. Park activities are referred to current Sabah Parks information, not unverified permits, schedules or guarantees.
